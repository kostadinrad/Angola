II. Executive Summary

Angola’s gambling sector operates under the Gambling Activity Law (Law No. 5/16), which reserves the right to exploit gambling activities to the State and channels private participation through concession agreements and licenses.[web:13] The sector regulator, the Instituto de Supervisão de Jogos (ISJ), under the Ministry of Finance, supervises casinos, social betting, lotteries, remote games and promotional prize contests, and has recently tightened enforcement and formalised new products such as the Loto 5/90 “Jogos Sociais” lottery game.[web:2][web:8][web:13]

The National Lottery was relaunched in Luanda in 2025 with an explicit emphasis on transparency, inclusion and social responsibility, with the State positioning itself as regulator, supervisor and inspector while opening operational space for experienced private partners.[web:5][web:15][web:22] ISJ data for 2025 show record gaming tax revenues of about Kz 28.2 billion (≈26M EUR), up roughly 60% year‑on‑year, driven in particular by growth in territorial social betting and formalisation of the market.[web:7][web:19] This context creates a timely window to position a private operator as a technology and operations partner for social games, lottery and scratchcards under a service‑ or concession‑type framework aligned with government policy.[web:5][web:13][web:19]

III. Core Regulatory and Market Picture

The Ministry of Finance is the political authority overseeing gambling policy and taxation, while the ISJ acts as the specialised regulator for gambling activities, including casinos, lotteries, betting, promotional games, raffles and remote games.[web:13][web:25] The general rule under Law No. 5/16 is that the right to operate gambling is reserved to the Angolan State, but this right can be granted to private entities via concession agreements, which define scope, duration and obligations.[web:13]

  • Instituto de Supervisão de Jogos (ISJ): Central regulator for all games of chance, social betting, lotteries, raffles and contests; it manages licensing, supervises compliance and has recently issued strict guidance that any raffle or prize‑based promotion must receive prior approval at least 30 days before launch through the SSJ online platform.[web:8][web:11][web:13]
  • National Lottery / Jogos Sociais: The National Lottery, relaunched in 2025, is framed as a symbol of transparency and social responsibility, with revenues earmarked for social and development programs while leveraging private‑sector expertise for operations under state oversight.[web:5][web:15][web:22]
  • Market Scale & Direction: Official data indicate record tax revenues of roughly Kz 28.2 billion from gambling activities in 2025, a 60% increase versus 2024, with particularly strong growth in territorial social betting and remote games, signalling both market potential and a willingness to formalise previously informal activity.[web:7][web:19]

IV. Lottery & Scratchcard Business Landscape

Angola’s National Lottery and “Jogos Sociais” are positioned as state‑backed social games whose proceeds support priority social and development programs, with the government explicitly calling for high standards of transparency, inclusion and ethical conduct.[web:5][web:15][web:22] In parallel, the Ministry of Finance has formalised specific products such as Loto 5/90 via Executive Decree No. 695/25, defining rules on organisation, participation, prize allocation and compliance, which provides a concrete template for future lottery and instant‑win offerings.[web:2][web:28][web:31]

  • State Domain with Private Operations: Lottery, social games and other gambling activities are in principle a state monopoly, but private companies can obtain concessions or licenses to operate under ISJ supervision and contractual arrangements with the State.[web:13][web:15]
  • Move to Formalise “Jogos Sociais”: Social betting and territorial social games have become a major contributor to gaming tax revenue, with 2025 figures showing almost 400% growth in this segment in the fourth quarter alone, highlighting strong demand and the impact of formalisation.[web:7][web:19]
  • Opportunity for Scratchcards: While territorial social betting and number games (e.g., Loto 5/90) are gaining traction, there is still space to structure a modern scratchcard portfolio and related draw‑based games with robust controls, aligning with the government’s emphasis on transparency and social returns.[web:2][web:5][web:19]

V. Online & Digital Channels

Angola’s Gambling Activity Law treats online and remote gambling as regulated activities, meaning remote games require authorisation and fall under ISJ supervision, rather than occupying an unregulated grey zone.[web:9][web:13] The number of licensed entities includes both land‑based and remote operators, with ISJ data indicating 24 licensed operators and 257 physical shops or payment points by late 2025, many linked to social betting operations.[web:7][web:19]

  • Regulated but Developing: Remote / online games are legally recognised but the framework is still evolving in practice, creating space to propose controlled pilots for digital lottery ticket sales and instant‑win games integrated with the SSJ supervision platform.[web:9][web:13][web:25]
  • Regulatory Digitalisation: ISJ has implemented the online Sistema de Supervisão de Jogos (SSJ) for submitting and monitoring approvals (e.g., for raffles and prize contests), signalling a broader digitalisation trend that can be leveraged for real‑time reporting and oversight of lottery and scratchcard operations.[web:8][web:11][web:25]
  • Convergence with Social Policy: Digital lottery products can be framed as a tool for widening controlled access while maintaining strong age‑verification, AML and responsible gaming mechanisms aligned with Angola’s broader financial‑sector risk assessments.[web:19][web:25]

VI. Compliance Function & Governance

A strong Regulatory Research & Compliance Officer role is critical for entering and operating in Angola, bridging internal standards with local legal requirements and supervisory expectations from ISJ, the Ministry of Finance and data‑protection authorities.[web:13][web:25] The function must map gambling, tax and data‑protection rules to concrete product, process and IT controls across both retail and digital channels.[web:13]

  • Regulatory Mapping: Analyse the Gambling Activity Law (Law No. 5/16), subsequent regulations (including Executive Decree No. 695/25 on Loto 5/90 and Law No. 17/24 on Gaming Activities where relevant), ISJ communiqués on raffles and prize contests, and applicable tax rules, then translate them into product‑specific requirements for lotto, scratchcards, social betting and promotions.[web:2][web:8][web:11][web:13]
  • Policy Framework: Build AML/CFT, KYC, responsible gaming, marketing and data‑protection policies aligned with Angolan law and ISJ expectations, including prior authorisation workflows for prize promotions and clear parameters for prize payouts and documentation.[web:8][web:11][web:13][web:25]
  • Monitoring & Reporting: Establish systems to deliver the data and reporting cadence ISJ expects (e.g., real‑time or near‑real‑time transactional data through secure interfaces, plus periodic regulatory and tax reports) and to monitor changes in laws, decrees and budget measures that affect gaming taxation and compliance obligations.[web:10][web:12][web:19][web:25]
  • Training & Culture: Provide continuous training for operations, retail, marketing and customer‑service teams on local rules (e.g., age limits, promotion approvals, AML indicators), embedding compliance as a core part of the partnership narrative with the government.[web:11][web:13]

What fit‑and‑proper, technical and financial suitability criteria will apply to shareholders, directors and key staff of the private operator under a concession or service agreement?[web:13]

B. Financial & Tax Structure

Given recent reforms and revenue growth, are any adjustments to lottery and social‑games tax structures being studied as part of the broader fiscal agenda, and how might a long‑term partnership influence this discussion?[web:14][web:19]
How are proceeds currently allocated between prizes, State beneficiaries and operator costs in existing National Lottery and “Jogos Sociais” schemes, and how much flexibility is there to optimise payout ratios, prize structures and margins for new scratchcard products?[web:5][web:15][web:22]
Does the government prefer a pure service‑fee model, a revenue‑share based on GGR, a minimum‑guarantee plus variable component, or other hybrid structure for a private partner supporting the National Lottery?

C. Online & Digital Transition
